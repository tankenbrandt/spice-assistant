02 · Prelab

Hand analysis says ±1.5 V.

Ideal diodes clip at the battery voltage. That's the number that goes in the prelab.

Prelab · question 2
D1 on when v_out > V1 D2 on when v_out < −V2 so clip at V1 = +1.5 V and −V2 = −1.5 V
03 · Run it

The simulator says ±1.99 V.

Same circuit with a real 1N4001 model. The extra 0.49 V is the diode's own forward drop. Better to find that out now than at the bench.

>

04 · Check the wiring

Miswire it, and it says so.

Batteries left out: the output clips at ±0.51 V and misses the spec's 1.7 to 2.4 V band. A one-second check before you re-patch the breadboard.

PowerShell
>
clipper_no_batteries.cir: FAIL
measured input peak: 5 V (-0.0%); positive clip: 0.5113 V; negative clip: -0.5113 V
05 · Which knob

Clip level = V1 + 0.49 V.

Sweeping V1 shows the rule, so you start from a calculated value instead of trial and error. Want a 2.5 V clip? Start at V1 = 2.0 V.

06 · Real parts

Your reading won't be 1.99 V.

08 · What this is

A pre-lab check, not a replacement for the bench.

It helps you

  • Predict what the bench will show
  • Explain theory vs. measurement in the report
  • Catch wiring mistakes before you build
  • Pick a starting value, not a guess

It doesn't

  • Replace measuring the real circuit
  • Make an AI-written circuit right. It only catches the wrong ones
  • Know your actual parts
I also tested the AI with it. Benchmark of 8 circuits: 100% simulate, 62–75% meet spec. Lab round: 9 of 10 correct; the miss was a backwards clamper diode, caught by the spec.
